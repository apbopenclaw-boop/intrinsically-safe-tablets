Three decisions that fail procurement review.

Most tablet procurement fails on zone, standard, or gas-group mismatch.

Zone 1 vs Zone 2.

Zone 1 = explosive atmosphere likely during normal ops. Zone 2 = only under abnormal conditions. A Zone 1 tablet covers both zones. A Zone 2 tablet cannot enter Zone 1 areas. Most process-area deployments require Zone 1 certification.

ATEX vs IECEx.

ATEX is mandatory in the EU/EEA. IECEx is the international scheme accepted in 50+ countries. For global deployments across multiple regions, choose dual-certified tablets. Many vendors certify under both frameworks.

IIA, IIB, IIC.

Gas groups classify ignition risk. IIC (hydrogen) is the hardest to certify for and covers IIA + IIB. If your site handles hydrogen or acetylene, the tablet must be IIC rated. Most modern Ex tablets target IIC for maximum site flexibility.

iPad-class hardware vs dedicated Ex tablets.

Dedicated Android and Windows Ex tablets have the longer certification track record. iPad-based solutions differ from them on three measurable points.

Processing power.

The Apple M4/M5 in the iPad Pro is desktop-class silicon. Dedicated Android Ex tablets use mid-range chipsets, which handle forms and checklists but are slower for 3D models, digital twins, LiDAR capture and on-device AI workloads.

Software lifetime.

Apple supports iPads with roughly 6–7 years of iPadOS upgrades and security updates beyond that. Android Ex tablets commonly ship with an already-older Android version and shorter update windows tied to chipset end-of-life — in 2026, current models still run Android 12/13. A longer secure lifetime spreads the certification premium over more years.

Display quality.

The iPad Pro's tandem OLED reaches 1,600 nits peak at 120 Hz, which helps in direct sunlight; most dedicated Ex tablets use TFT or IPS panels. The Getac F110-Ex has the largest panel in this comparison (11.6″).

Certification status: the Xshielder iPad Pro 11 holds an ATEX Zone 1/21 certificate. Atexxo's converted iPad 11″ for Zone 1 is announced. Match the certificate marking to your site's zone classification before deployment.

Field tablet use cases, honestly.

Tablets in hazardous areas serve different roles than phones. Larger screens enable workflows that phones can't.

Inspection & reporting.

Primary use case

  • Form-based inspection checklists on 8–10″ screens
  • Photo documentation with annotation
  • PDF/drawing review in the field
  • Barcode/QR scanning for asset tracking

Process & operations.

Growing use case

  • SCADA/HMI dashboards at the process unit
  • Digital work orders and permit-to-work
  • Video calls for remote expert support
  • ERP/SAP access in hazardous zones

Questions, answered.

What is an intrinsically safe tablet?

An intrinsically safe tablet is a mobile device certified under ATEX or IECEx for use in explosive atmospheres. It limits electrical energy so it cannot produce sparks or heat that could ignite flammable gas or dust. Certification covers Zone 1 (explosive atmosphere likely during normal operation) or Zone 2 (only under abnormal conditions).

What is the difference between Zone 1 and Zone 2 tablets?

Zone 1 tablets are certified for areas where an explosive gas atmosphere is likely during normal operation. Zone 2 tablets are certified only for areas where it is unlikely and short-lived. A Zone 1 tablet can be used in Zone 1 and Zone 2; a Zone 2 tablet cannot be used in Zone 1.

Can I use a regular tablet in a hazardous area?

No. Uncertified electronic equipment in a classified hazardous area breaches ATEX Directive 1999/92/EC (worker protection) and national rules such as DSEAR in the UK. A standard tablet can produce sparks or surface temperatures that ignite an explosive atmosphere. Only ATEX or IECEx certified equipment may be used.

Is there an ATEX-certified iPad for Zone 1?

Yes. For the full-size iPad Pro 11″, the Xshielder Ex db case (II 2G Ex db IIC T6 Gb) is ATEX Zone 1/21 certified. For the iPad mini 4 to 7, Xciel ships ATEX/IECEx Zone 1 flameproof enclosures (XCZ1, factory-fitted). Atexxo's converted iPad 11″ for Zone 1 is announced. For Zone 2/22, Atexxo sells converted iPads and Xciel sells C1D2/Zone 2 cases.

What does Gas Group IIC mean for tablets?

Gas Group IIC is the most demanding gas group under IEC 60079 and covers hydrogen and acetylene. A tablet certified for IIC also covers IIA (e.g. propane) and IIB (e.g. ethylene).
